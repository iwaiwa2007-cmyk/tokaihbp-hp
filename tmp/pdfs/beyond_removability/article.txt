            Malignant hilar biliary obstruction (MHBO) remains one of the most technically
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            demanding conditions in therapeutic pancreatobiliary endoscopy. Although bilateral
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            drainage using uncovered self-expandable metal stents (UMSs) has conventionally been
                                                                                  
            favored because of their prolonged patency and preservation of side-branch drainage, the
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            non-removability of UMSs represents an increasingly important limitation.
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            Improvements in systemic therapy have prolonged survival in patients with biliary tract
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            and other malignancies, and recurrent biliary obstruction (RBO) after UMS placement is
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            therefore encountered more frequently. Once tumor ingrowth occurs, reintervention
                                                                                  
            through previously deployed UMSs can be technically challenging, particularly when
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            selective access to an undrained or obstructed segment is required.   
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            Inoue et al. recently compared suprapapillary stent-by-stent deployment of 6-mm slim
                                                                                  
            fully covered metal stents (FCMSs) with conventional UMS deployment for unresectable
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            MHBO in a multicenter retrospective study [1]. After propensity score matching, 52
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            patients were included in each group. Technical and clinical success rates were
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            comparable, whereas the FCMS group had a significantly lower incidence of RBO than
                                                                                  
            the UMS group (32.0% vs. 60.8%) and a significantly longer time to RBO. Importantly,
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            FCMSs could be removed in all patients undergoing reintervention. The reintervention

                                                               Page 6 of 12       
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            time was shorter, and both the total number of reinterventions and the proportion of
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            patients requiring multiple reinterventions were lower with FCMSs.    
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            The study provides clinically relevant evidence supporting a conceptual shift from
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            “permanent drainage” to “removable drainage” in MHBO. The principal advantage of an
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            FCMS is not merely the prevention of tumor ingrowth. When RBO occurs, removal of
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            the indwelling stents can restore access to the biliary confluence and allow the drainage
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            strategy to be reconstructed according to the subsequent anatomical and oncological
                                                                                  
            situation. This feature may be particularly valuable in patients expected to survive long
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            enough to require repeated interventions and in the increasingly important subgroup
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            undergoing conversion surgery after successful systemic treatment.    
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            Suprapapillary placement may further improve the performance of slim FCMSs.
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            Avoiding direct exposure of the stent lumen to duodenal contents could reduce
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            duodenobiliary reflux and sludge formation, while the suprapapillary position may
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            decrease distal migration into the duodenum. Nevertheless, the present study also clearly
                                                                                  
            illustrates that removability does not eliminate the inherent disadvantages of covering the
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            hilar biliary tree. Two patients in the FCMS group developed posterior-segment bilomas

            within 2 months, necessitating stent removal. In both cases, the proximal end of the right-
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            sided FCMS was positioned at the bifurcation of the anterior and posterior sectoral ducts.
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            Although a definite obstruction was not demonstrated, coverage of the posterior sectoral
                                                                                  
            branch or disturbance of localized bile flow was strongly suspected. Thus, the proximal
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            end of a covered stent may be as important as the stricture itself.   
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            Branch occlusion can manifest not only as overt cholangitis but also as segmental
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            cholangitis, liver abscess, or biloma. These events may be difficult to predict because
                                                                                  
            apparently adequate contrast drainage at the end of the procedure does not necessarily
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            guarantee sustained drainage after full stent expansion. Placing the proximal stent end
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            precisely at a major bifurcation may be particularly hazardous. When both the anterior
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            and posterior sectoral ducts remain patent, either complete bridging of the bifurcation or
                                                                                  
            separate drainage using three stents may be preferable to leaving the covered stent edge
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            at the bifurcation. Preprocedural assessment of the target liver volume and detailed
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            interpretation of three-dimensional biliary anatomy are therefore essential.
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            Could multi-hole fully covered self-expandable metal stents (MHSEMSs) provide the
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            next step in this evolution? MHSEMSs incorporate multiple small holes into the covering
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  

=== PAGE 8 ===
                                                                                  
                                                               Page 8 of 12       
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            membrane, theoretically allowing bile to flow from adjacent branches while retaining
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            many advantages of a covered stent. The holes may also permit limited tissue anchoring
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            and thereby reduce migration. Successful suprapapillary stent-in-stent and side-by-side
                                                                                  
            deployment of MHSEMSs for MHBO has already been described, demonstrating the
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            technical feasibility of bilateral drainage while maintaining potential removability [2, 3].
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            A recent porcine hilar-obstruction model also suggested preservation of side-branch
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            drainage and successful removability after MHSEMS placement [4].      
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            Clinical data from malignant distal biliary obstruction provide further proof of concept.
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            In a multicenter retrospective study of 111 patients, the migration rate after MHSEMS
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            placement was only 1.9%, and successful removal was achieved in 88.9% of attempted
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            cases [5]. These findings suggest that a multi-hole design might address two important
                                                                                  
            FCMS-related problems: side-branch obstruction and migration. In the context of the
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            current study, communication through the holes might preserve drainage of the posterior
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            sectoral duct even when the stent edge or covered membrane overlaps the bifurcation,
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            potentially reducing the risks of segmental cholangitis and biloma.   
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            However, MHSEMSs should not yet be regarded as a definitive solution. The same holes

            that permit branch drainage and tissue anchoring may allow tumor or hyperplastic tissue
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            to grow into the stent. Indeed, ingrowth has been reported after MHSEMS placement for
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            distal malignant biliary obstruction [5]. Another comparative study in pancreatic cancer
                                                                                  
            found a shorter time to RBO with MHSEMSs than with conventional FCMSs,
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            emphasizing that reduced migration does not necessarily translate into superior overall
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            patency [6]. The optimal number, diameter, and distribution of the holes remain
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            undetermined, and whether they provide sufficient drainage when a major sectoral duct
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            is directly covered has not been established. Moreover, clinical evidence for MHBO is
                                                                                  
            currently limited primarily to technical reports, small case series, and preclinical data.
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            The ideal stent for MHBO should simultaneously prevent tumor ingrowth, preserve side-
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            branch drainage, resist migration, facilitate bilateral or multisegmental deployment, and
                                                                                  
            remain completely removable. Conventional UMSs preserve branch drainage but are not
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            removable; standard FCMSs are removable but may occlude branches and migrate.
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            MHSEMSs represent a rational attempt to reconcile these conflicting requirements, but
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            their benefits and trade-offs require prospective evaluation.         
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            Inoue et al. have shown that suprapapillary slim FCMS placement can reduce RBO and

            substantially simplify reintervention compared with UMS placement. Their study also
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            identifies the remaining obstacle: safe preservation of segmental bile flow. Future trials
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            should compare standard slim FCMSs, MHSEMSs, and contemporary UMSs using not
                                                                                  
            only time to RBO but also segment-specific adverse events, removability, success of
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            selective reintervention, cumulative procedure burden, and compatibility with subsequent
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            surgery. The next advance in MHBO drainage may depend less on whether a stent is
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
            covered or uncovered than on how intelligently its covering is designed.
